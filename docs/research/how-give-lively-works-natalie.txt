  Summary
  We are building a gala operations tool that sits between Give Lively and gala night, and it
  does not replace Give Lively. AAFE keeps selling tickets and sponsorships through Give
  Lively, which is free, already embedded on aafe.org and already sends tax receipts. Our
  tool handles the four things nothing in their stack does: collecting each guest's name for a
  purchase, assigning tables, tracking payment across Give Lively, checks, direct deposits
  and comps, and checking guests in at the door. It has two doors, a staff app and a guest-
  facing names form. The hardest problem is not technical. It is timing: once a buyer finishes
  checkout without naming their guests, getting those names costs staff weeks of email
  and phone calls.


  How Give Lively works for AAFE
  Give Lively is a free fundraising platform for nonprofits, and AAFE uses it as its existing
  fundraising platform, named as such in the project intake form.

  Give Lively pricing · Give Lively integrations

  One thing to confirm with AAFE: the intake form refers to "GiveLively's current offering
  under our subscription," but Give Lively has no subscription. The paid platform in their
  stack may be Salesforce, an add-on or an event producer.


  Why AAFE should keep Give Lively
  Nothing in the intake form asks anyone to replace Give Lively. It asks for a tool that works
  with it, and that is the right read.

        It costs nothing, so replacing it saves no money.
        Tax receipts are handled automatically, and getting receipts wrong has consequences
        for donors and for AAFE.
        The embedded forms already keep buyers on aafe.org, so a branded checkout is not a
        reason to move.
        Donor records already flow into Salesforce, if the sync is switched on.
        Year-round donations run through it, not just the gala. Moving the gala off Give Lively
        would split AAFE's giving across two systems.

  The five gaps in the intake form
  Every constraint Janelle lists is about guests, not about money moving.

   1. Guest names per package. Sponsorship levels and ticket packages come with
      different numbers of guests, so collecting names needs conditional questions that
      Give Lively does not offer.
   2. Sponsors cannot edit. Once a sponsor adds a guest name, Give Lively does not
      support changing it.
   3. Buyer is not the attendee. A coordinator often buys for a manager or colleagues, so
      contact information and attendee information have to be tracked separately.
   4. Payments arrive by several routes. AAFE encourages the event website, but also
      accepts sponsorship dollars by check and direct deposit, which means manual
        additions to the guest list.
   5. Comped guests. A number of tickets, such as those for elected officials, carry no
      payment but still need RSVP tracking.

  Two more requirements sit alongside these: each guest needs an AAFE relationship
  owner, and sponsors need a marketing materials status for logos and program ads.


  The core constraint: the moment of purchase
  There is one moment when a buyer is paying full attention to AAFE: checkout. They have
  their card out, they are filling in a form, and they expect to be asked things. That is the
  cheapest possible moment to collect ten guest names.

  Give Lively cannot ask at that moment, because it cannot vary the questions by package.
  So the names have to be collected afterward, and everything gets harder:

        Attention drops to near zero. The buyer has done what they came to do. A follow-up
        email arrives days later, competing with everything else in their inbox.
        The buyer often does not know yet. A company that buys a table in February may not
        decide who is attending until April.
        Staff absorb the cost. Chasing names becomes email, then a second email, then a
        phone call, then a call to an assistant, repeated across dozens of sponsors during the
        busiest weeks before the event.

        The deadline is immovable. Name tags print on a fixed date. Seating has to be final
        before that. Every unreturned name compresses the same fixed window.

  This is why the intake form describes staff time as the problem, not software. The
  spreadsheet is bulky because it is a chase list.

  Our tool cannot remove this constraint while Give Lively handles checkout. What it can do
  is make the chase cheap: know exactly who is missing what, sort it by the staff member
  who owns the relationship, and draft the follow-up in one click. If AAFE ever wants the
  constraint removed rather than managed, that requires asking for names at checkout,
  which is the decision laid out below.


  The two tools
  One product, one database, two doors. Who is looking decides what they see.

  Door 1: the staff guest list app. AAFE staff and gala volunteers sign in and work the whole
  event.

        Overview of progress toward sponsorship, ticket and attendance goals
        Every guest with missing information, and what is missing
        Guests grouped by AAFE relationship owner
        One-click follow-up email drafts, copied out for a person to send
        Drag-and-drop seating chart with table capacity
        Payments across Give Lively, check, direct deposit, comp and sponsor-included
        Logo and program ad status for sponsors
        Check-in on gala night, recording who checked each guest in
        Import from a Give Lively export, matching existing guests rather than duplicating
        them

  Door 2: the guest names form. The person who bought tickets opens a private link and
  sees only their own purchase.

        The right number of name slots for their package, which is gap 1
        Editable until a deadline AAFE sets, which is gap 2
        Buyer details kept separate from attendee details, which is gap 3
        Their own payment status and table number, once assigned
        The option to cancel

  Scenarios
  Seven ways a guest reaches the gala, and what the tool does in each. Every one of these
  exists in the intake form or follows from it.

  1. A sponsor buys a table on Give Lively. Helen buys a $50,000 Lead Sponsorship for
  twelve seats. Give Lively records the purchase and emails her a receipt. Our tool picks up
  the purchase, creates twelve seats, marks eleven names missing, and sends Helen a link
  to name her guests. Her AAFE owner sees the gap on the overview.

  2. A coordinator buys for someone else. An assistant buys two tickets for her firm's
  partners and is not attending. The buyer and the attendees are separate records. All
  correspondence goes to the assistant; the name tags print the partners' names.

  3. A sponsorship arrives by check. A bank mails a $25,000 check with no Give Lively
  record at all. Staff add the sponsorship in one step, mark the method as check, and the
  same guest-name link goes out. On the overview it counts toward the sponsorship goal
  exactly like a card payment.

  4. A comped guest. An elected official is invited with no payment expected. Staff add
  them as a comp, so they take a seat and appear at check-in, but never appear as a balance
  owed or in a payment reminder.

  5. A sponsor who does not respond. The common case. Two weeks pass, the seats stay
  unnamed. The tool shows exactly which sponsors are outstanding, how many names each
  still owes, and who at AAFE owns that relationship, and drafts the reminder email
  addressed to the buyer rather than the unnamed seat. Staff still make the phone call, but
  they make it from a list instead of a spreadsheet hunt.

  6. A change the week of the gala. A sponsor swaps two guests three days out. They open
  their link and edit the names themselves, which Give Lively does not allow. The seating
  chart and name tag list update immediately.

  7. Gala night. Staff check guests in on a phone or tablet. Someone arrives who is not on
  the list and is added on the spot. At the end, AAFE knows who actually attended rather
  than who bought a ticket, which is information that has never survived a gala before.

  can trigger a Stripe review either way.


  What happens to the data afterward
  Give Lively knows what happened on its website. It does not know what happened in the
  room, because that information never passed through it.

  Four things are created during gala preparation and on gala night, and none of them reach
  Salesforce today:

        who actually attended, as opposed to who bought a ticket
        which guests a sponsor brought
        who sat at which table
        dietary needs and any accommodations

  They live in the gala spreadsheet, and nobody is available to retype a 600-person
  spreadsheet into Salesforce after the event. The file gets saved, becomes next year's
  template, and each gala effectively starts over. That is why questions like "who has
  attended three years running but never given on their own?" cannot be answered.

  Our tool captures all four as a by-product of doing its job, so a Salesforce-ready export at
  the end costs almost nothing. A direct Salesforce integration is possible but out of scope
  for a capstone: it needs API access, an administrator's permissions and knowledge of
  AAFE's customizations. An export gets most of the value.

  Worth confirming with AAFE rather than assuming: some of this may already be recorded
  by hand.



  What the demo shows, and what it hides
  The Demo Day build runs on invented data, and the flow will look smoother than a real
  gala.

        Everyone responds instantly. In the demo, a fake sponsor clicks the link and fills in
        names right away. In April, a third of them will not have answered for weeks. The mock
        data should deliberately include unresponsive sponsors so the reminder tooling has
        something to do.
        No real money moves. The checkout is a working demonstration, not a live payment.
        The code, the card form and the confirmation are all simulated.
        No live Give Lively connection. Purchases arrive through a file import. An automatic
        feed would need Zapier and a hosted address, which belongs in a later phase.
        The spreadsheet is a guess. Without a sample of AAFE's real guest spreadsheet, the
        fields are inferred from the intake form.

  Naming these in the presentation is better than having someone find them. It shows we
  understand the difference between a demo and an operation.

  Open questions for AAFE
  For the call on October 1.

  The guest list today

        Can we see last year's guest spreadsheet, redacted, or at least its column headers?
        Without it we are guessing at fields.
        How do you get guest names from sponsors today, and how many follow-ups does it
        usually take?
        What are the deadlines for guest names, sponsor materials, seating and name tag
        printing?

  Systems and cost

        The intake form mentions a subscription. Which platforms are you paying for, and
        roughly what does the gala stack cost each year?
        Is the Give Lively to Salesforce sync switched on? If so, we may read from Salesforce
        instead.
        Who administers Salesforce, and does the gala guest list end up there afterward?
        Was the 2026 ticket page on Give Lively, and can we see an export from it?

  The event

        Who builds the seating plan, and when? We understand the venue leaves it to the
        client.
        What are this year's sponsorship, ticket and attendance goals?
        How quickly do gala funds reach your bank account today, and has a payout ever been
        delayed?
        Is it important that guests stay on an AAFE-branded page when they buy?

  After the project

        Who at AAFE would own this tool day to day, and are there limits on ongoing costs?
        Who owns the code and intellectual property we build?
